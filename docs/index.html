    How it works, and how well

    Matching across score scales

    ICLR changed its scale every year (2024-25: 1/3/5/6/8/10, 2026: 0-10 even, 2027: 1-4), so raw numbers can't be compared. Every score is turned into its percentile among all reviews that year, and every paper into three numbers: the percentile of its mean, of its lowest score and of its highest score. The {K} nearest papers of each past year are its twins (all ties included).

    Backtest on years it never saw

    Settings were tuned with 2024 predicting 2025, then tested once with 2024-25 predicting all of 2026.

    Limits

    • These are rates for past papers that looked like yours, not a prediction for your paper. The AC reads the reviews, the rebuttal and the paper; none of that is in the numbers.
    • 2027 percentiles use the 2027 scores as they are today, which still move during the rebuttal.
    • Reviewer replies and rebuttal length are only public for 2024. They show association, not cause: a long rebuttal may be a sign of a hard review, not a cause of the outcome.
    • Withdrawn papers count as their own outcome. Desk rejects are left out of the AC numbers. ICLR 2026 had no spotlight tier.
    • Code links added during the rebuttal are not in any public dataset, so they are not used.
